This study shows that preschool children (ages 3-5) evaluate moral actions not only by outcomes but also by the alternatives available at the time of choice. Using a child-friendly trolley problem, children judged an action as morally good when no better alternative existed, but as morally bad when a non-harmful alternative was available but not chosen. This indicates that even young children consider the context of available choices in moral judgments.

Why it matters: It provides experimental evidence on early developmental aspects of moral reasoning and decision-making, relevant to evolutionary psychology and cognitive neuroscience.

Occam: MLP framework for cell representation and perturbation effect prediction

Occam is a multilayer perceptron model combining denoised cell representations with gene interaction, text, and protein features to predict genetic perturbation effects. It excels in few-shot cross-context prediction where perturbation and cell context are seen separately but not jointly. Occam outperforms retrained competitors and direct transfer methods, better capturing combined double perturbation responses. Modeling quantum information scrambling in protein NMR experiments

This work develops a computational pipeline linking protein molecular structures to out-of-time-order correlator (OTOC) dynamics measurable by NMR. Incorporating molecular dynamics, orientational averaging, and spin-network truncation, it analyzes over 350 carbon-carbon spin pairs across 10 proteins. OTOC arrival times scale linearly with interspin distance, revealing operator-spreading velocities enabling millisecond-scale correlations. Sensitivity analysis shows OTOCs constrain primarily interspin distances, highlighting their potential for probing spatially extended spin correlations in solid-state NMR.

Why it matters It bridges quantum information concepts with experimental NMR, enabling computational genomics researchers to explore spatial chromatin and protein interaction dynamics.

Expressivity of 2D Gaussian fermion tensor network states linked to entanglement area law

This study proves that for 2D translation-invariant Gaussian fermionic states (single-band BCS superconductors and two-band insulators), an area law for Renyi-0 entropy is equivalent to exact finite bond dimension Gaussian fermionic tensor network state (TNS) representations.
